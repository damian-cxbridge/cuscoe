# IT

IT owns the infrastructure: cloud environments, any on-premise hardware and software, and their security. That covers access control, firewalls, encryption, data classification and audit trails.

## Tool and model selection

The [Centres of Excellence](centres-of-excellence.md) choose the models and AI tools they use, based on what customers need. IT reviews each choice to make sure that:

- security has been accounted for
- the tool or architecture fits the existing estate
- infrastructure costs are allocated correctly
- the vendor's documentation and service levels allow security, compliance or regulatory failures to be passed back to the vendor on terms acceptable to the business and its customers

[Compliance](legal-and-compliance.md) separately confirms the choice meets regulatory standards.

## Neither side has a veto

IT and the Centres of Excellence sit at the same level, and neither can veto the other. A hard IT veto would slow the business down every time it needs a new capability. No escalation route at all would leave security exposed to decisions made by a single centre.

Most reviews are settled directly between IT and the team requesting the tool. When they cannot agree:

- security, compliance or regulatory issues go to the board-level AI owner, who decides whether to accept, mitigate or reject the onboarding
- cost issues go to [finance](finance.md), which decides whether the risk is acceptable, and owns the resulting margin impact if it accepts it. If finance does not accept the risk, IT escalates to the board-level AI owner.

Escalation should be the exception, not the norm.
