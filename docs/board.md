# Board

The board sits outside the day-to-day model. It has one line into the model, through Portfolio governance and AI risk, and no direct role in the daily loop between functions and customers.

## What the board holds

- **A named AI owner.** One person at board level is accountable for AI and for the operating model. This might be a Chief AI Officer or an existing board member such as the CEO, MD or COO.
- **AI risk on the risk register.**
- **Approval of the AI governance policy.**
- **Final accountability for the [Portfolio](portfolio.md).**

## Why AI cannot belong to one function

AI runs through every part of the model: delivery, pricing, finance, contracts, compliance and infrastructure. If one function owns it, the business sees AI through that function's priorities. Ownership therefore sits at board level, and day-to-day responsibilities are spread across the functions through a RACI matrix, with each function accountable for AI within its own area.

## The final escalation point

The board-level AI owner resolves the tensions that the functions cannot settle between themselves. The main routes are:

| Disagreement | Who decides |
|---|---|
| A vendor will not accept terms that protect the business | Board-level AI owner, escalated by legal |
| Legal and compliance disagree on whether a risk is acceptable | Board-level AI owner |
| IT and a requesting team cannot agree on a security, compliance or regulatory issue | Board-level AI owner |
| IT and a requesting team cannot agree on cost | Finance, then the board-level AI owner if finance will not accept the risk |
| Portfolio trade-offs between functions | Portfolio executive sponsor, with the board accountable |
| Other parts of the business bypass the skill matrix | Centre of Excellence Director |

## Evidence status

Evaluated against an illustrative scenario. Not yet tested in a live organisation.
